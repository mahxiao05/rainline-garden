      What your zone tells you

      What the number means

      It's how cold the coldest winter night usually gets, averaged over 1991 to 2020. Each zone covers 10°F; "a" is the colder 5°F half, "b" the warmer. Zone 8b–9a means about 15 to 25°F.

      It's an average, so a hard winter can still go colder. Give borderline plants a sheltered spot.

      It's not a planting date

      Your zone says which perennials, shrubs and trees survive winter. It says nothing about summer length: Hillsboro and Beaverton and Port Townsend are both zone 8b–9a, but have about 190 and 272 frost-free days.

      For when to plant, use your town calendar.

      What changed in 2023

      The 2023 USDA map replaced the 2012 one, with newer weather records and more stations. Many places moved half a zone warmer, so an old plant tag may list your zone one step colder.
