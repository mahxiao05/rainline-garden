    Gardening in the Oregon Coast. Cool, foggy summers and steady wind hold crops back more than frost does. Shelter warm crops from the wind, pick early, cool-tolerant varieties, and expect salt spray in gardens right by the shore.

    Frost date isn't planting date. The typical last frost in Coos Bay and North Bend is February 21, but tomatoes wait until May 20, about 13 weeks later, because the soil and nights are still too cold for them. Why soil temperature matters.

    How the season in Coos Bay and North Bend compares

    With 291 frost-free days, Coos Bay and North Bend have the 2nd-longest season of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, February 21, is in the middle of the towns we cover in the Oregon Coast: 21 days later than Brookings and Gold Beach and 60 days earlier than Tillamook.

    Late frosts are harder to predict here than in most towns. There are 32 days between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10, against 19 for a typical town on this site, so keep frost cover handy until March 25.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 6 and set out kale plants by August 20. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (December 9 on average) and to shortening days.

    Where these dates come from

    The frost dates for Coos Bay and North Bend come from the NOAA weather station at North Bend Regional Airport, about 3 miles north of Coos Bay and about 1 mile northwest of North Bend, at 20 feet. It's the closest weather station to Coos Bay and North Bend with NOAA's 1991–2020 frost records. It's an airport station, out on open ground, so a garden in town, on a slope or in a low spot can run a few days earlier or later.

    They're NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals: 30-year averages, not a forecast. How we pick each town's station.

    Common questions about frost in Coos Bay and North Bend

    When is the last frost in Coos Bay and North Bend?

    February 21 in a typical year. Frost is over by March 25 in 9 years out of 10, Tender plants like tomatoes wait longer than that, for warm soil: see the tomato question below.

    When is the first frost in Coos Bay and North Bend?

    Around December 9. It comes as early as November 12 in 1 year out of 10.

    When can I plant tomatoes in Coos Bay and North Bend?

    Set out tomato plants May 20 – Jun 17, after starting seeds indoors Mar 25 – Apr 8.

    When should I plant garlic in Coos Bay and North Bend?

    Plant garlic cloves Oct 1 – Nov 15, and harvest next summer when the lower leaves turn brown.

    How long is the growing season in Coos Bay and North Bend?

    About 291 frost-free days, one of the longest of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover.
