    Burns, OR frost dates and planting calendar

    The typical last spring frost in Burns is June 11, and the first fall frost is around September 6. Plant peas from Apr 30; plant tomatoes from Jun 11.

    Last spring frostJune 11Typical date. Frost is over by Jun 28 in 9 years out of 10.
    First fall frostSeptember 6Typical date. Comes as early as Aug 21 in 1 year out of 10.
    Frost-free season87 daysYour growing season for frost-tender crops like tomatoes and squash.

    High desert and mountains. Central and high eastern Oregon. Frost is possible almost any month. Use row covers, wall-o-water or cloches for warm crops, and choose short-season varieties.

    Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Burns Municipal Airport (1262 m elevation).

    How the season in Burns compares

    With 87 frost-free days, Burns has the shortest season of the 71 Oregon and Washington towns we cover. The longest is Brookings and Gold Beach at 330 days.

    The typical last frost here, June 11, is the latest of the towns we cover in Central Oregon and the high desert: 23 days later than Madras and 2 days later than Bend and Redmond.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 17 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until June 28.

    The season is too short for winter squash, dahlias, melons, sweet potatoes and pumpkins to finish from a normal planting date. Grow short-season varieties, start them indoors early, or use a cold frame or row cover to buy time. Sweet corn, tomatoes, peppers, basil, eggplant, pole beans, sunflowers, tomatillos, zinnias and ground cherries also need most of the season.
