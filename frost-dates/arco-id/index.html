    About Arco

    Arco sits at about 5,300 feet at the edge of the Snake River Plain, near Craters of the Moon. In 1955 it became the first town lit by nuclear power.

    How the season in Arco compares

    With 123 frost-free days, Arco has the 100th-longest season of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, May 22, is in the middle of the towns we cover in the Magic Valley and eastern Idaho: 21 days later than Glenns Ferry and 22 days earlier than Driggs.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 18 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until June 9.

    Even here, winter squash, sweet potatoes and pumpkins need nearly the whole season, so plant them on time and pick shorter-season varieties when you can.

    Common questions about frost in Arco
    When is the last frost in Arco?

    May 22 in a typical year, and frost is over by June 9 in 9 years out of 10. Peas and other cool crops don't need to wait for it: peas can go in from April 20. Tomatoes, beans and squash wait longer than the frost date, until the soil warms (see the tomato question below).

    When is the first frost in Arco?

    Around September 22 in a typical year, and as early as September 4 in 1 year out of 10. From about Sep 4 on, watch the forecast and pick or cover tomatoes, peppers, squash and basil before a cold night. Kale, carrots, leeks and cabbage handle a light frost fine and keep going.

    When can I plant tomatoes in Arco?

    Set out tomato plants May 25 – Jun 22. Wait for soil around 60°F and nights near 50°F; tomatoes stall in cold soil. To grow your own from seed, start them indoors Mar 30 – Apr 13, or buy young plants at planting time. Keep frost cover handy until about Jun 9. The season is short here, so pick an early variety (under 70 days).

    When should I plant garlic in Arco?

    Plant garlic cloves Sep 20 – Oct 20, pointy end up, in a sunny bed. The cloves need a winter of cold to split into a full bulb, which is why fall planting works and spring planting often gives one small round bulb. Dig the next summer, once the lower leaves have turned brown.

    What growing zone is Arco in?

    USDA hardiness zone 5a, on the 2023 USDA map: the coldest winter night is usually −20 to −15°F. The zone tells you which perennials, shrubs and trees survive the winter. For vegetables it matters much less: go by the frost dates and planting calendar on this page instead.

    How long is the growing season in Arco?

    About 123 frost-free days, from May 22 to September 22 in a typical year. That's shorter than most of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. 19 plants on the calendar get a second planting for fall here, including radishes, spinach and lettuce.

    How much rain does Arco get?

    About 9 inches a year, counting melted snow. May is the wettest month and July the driest. That's dry country, so plan to water all summer; June to August brings just 2.0 inches. Figures are NOAA 1991–2020 normals.
