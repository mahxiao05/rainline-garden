    Ontario, OR frost dates and planting calendar

    The typical last spring frost in Ontario is April 21, and the first fall frost is around October 14. Plant peas from Mar 20; plant tomatoes from May 1.

    Last spring frostApril 21Typical date. Frost is over by May 8 in 9 years out of 10.
    First fall frostOctober 14Typical date. Comes as early as Sep 29 in 1 year out of 10.
    Frost-free season176 daysYour growing season for frost-tender crops like tomatoes and squash.

    Columbia Basin and low eastern valleys. Hot, dry summers and quick spring warm-up. Warm crops go in earlier than west of the Cascades, and irrigation matters more than rain.

    Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Ontario Municipal Airport (668 m elevation). Gardens in cold hollows or at higher elevation can frost later in spring and earlier in fall; city gardens and warm, south-facing spots often run a week or so ahead.

    New to gardening? Start with these 5 easy crops

    They're forgiving, quick and hard to kill in Ontario. Tap one for planting dates and step-by-step instructions, or read the beginner's guide.

    • RadishesReady in about four weeks, so you see results fast.
    • LettuceGrows in cool weather and a bit of shade, and you can pick it for weeks.
    • Bush beansBig seeds that are easy to plant, and one sowing feeds you all summer.
    • Zucchini and summer squashTwo plants are plenty. It almost always produces.
    • KaleShrugs off Northwest cold and keeps going into winter.
