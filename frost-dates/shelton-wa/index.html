    West-side valleys. The Willamette Valley, Puget Sound and southern Oregon valleys. Long frost-free seasons, but soil stays cold and wet into May, so warm crops wait for warm soil, not just the last frost.

    Gardening in Seattle and Puget Sound. Summers here are mild and rarely very hot, so heat lovers like peppers, melons and big tomatoes ripen slowly: give them your warmest, sunniest spot. Slugs love the wet months, and July and August are dry enough that beds need watering.

    Frost date isn't planting date. The typical last frost in Shelton is April 27, but tomatoes wait until May 15, about 3 weeks later, because the soil is still too cold for them to grow. Why soil temperature matters.

    How the season in Shelton compares

    With 172 frost-free days, Shelton has the 49th-longest season of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, April 27, is in the middle of the towns we cover in Seattle and Puget Sound: 56 days later than Seattle and Tacoma and 2 days earlier than Olympia.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 15 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until May 12.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 6 and set out kale plants by August 20. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (October 16 on average) and to shortening days.

    Where these dates come from

    The frost dates for Shelton come from the NOAA weather station at Shelton Sanderson Field, about 3 miles northwest of Shelton, at 270 feet. It's the closest weather station to Shelton with NOAA's 1991–2020 frost records. It's an airport station, out on open ground, so a garden in town, on a slope or in a low spot can run a few days earlier or later.

    They're NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals: 30-year averages, not a forecast. How we pick each town's station.

    Common questions about frost in Shelton

    When is the last frost in Shelton?

    April 27 in a typical year. Frost is over by May 12 in 9 years out of 10, Tender plants like tomatoes wait longer than that, for warm soil: see the tomato question below.

    When is the first frost in Shelton?

    Around October 16. It comes as early as October 1 in 1 year out of 10.

    When can I plant tomatoes in Shelton?

    Set out tomato plants May 15 – Jun 12, after starting seeds indoors Mar 20 – Apr 3.

    When should I plant garlic in Shelton?

    Plant garlic cloves Oct 1 – Nov 15, and harvest next summer when the lower leaves turn brown.

    How long is the growing season in Shelton?

    About 172 frost-free days, shorter than most of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover.
