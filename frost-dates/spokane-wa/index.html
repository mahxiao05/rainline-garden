    Spokane, WA frost dates and planting calendar

    The typical last spring frost in Spokane is April 26, and the first fall frost is around October 12. Plant peas from Apr 1; plant tomatoes from May 20.

    Last spring frostApril 26Typical date. Frost is over by May 10 in 9 years out of 10.
    First fall frostOctober 12Typical date. Comes as early as Sep 29 in 1 year out of 10.
    Frost-free season169 daysYour growing season for frost-tender crops like tomatoes and squash.

    Inland Northwest. Higher, colder interior valleys. Cold springs and an earlier fall mean a shorter season; quick-maturing varieties pay off.

    Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Spokane International Airport (717 m elevation).

    How the season in Spokane compares

    With 169 frost-free days, Spokane has the 47th-longest season of the 71 Oregon and Washington towns we cover. They run from 87 days in Burns to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, April 26, is the earliest of the towns we cover in Spokane and eastern Washington: 3 days earlier than Pullman and 6 days earlier than Colville.

    Spring here is steadier than in most towns: the last frost is over in 9 years out of 10 just 14 days after the typical date, against 20 for a typical town on this site.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 3 and set out kale plants by August 17. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (October 12 on average) and to shortening days.
