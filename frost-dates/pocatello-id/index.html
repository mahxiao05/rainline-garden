    Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Pocatello (4,460 ft elevation). These dates come from the weather station in town. The Pocatello airport, out on the plain northwest of town, runs about three weeks colder (its typical last frost is May 23), so gardens outside town should plan on later dates.

    How the season in Pocatello compares

    With 162 frost-free days, Pocatello has the 57th-longest season of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, May 2, is the earliest of the towns we cover in the Magic Valley and eastern Idaho: 5 days earlier than Burley and Rupert and 15 days earlier than Salmon.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 16 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until May 18.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 2 and set out kale plants by August 16. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (October 11 on average) and to shortening days.

    Common questions about frost in Pocatello

    When is the last frost in Pocatello?

    May 2 in a typical year. Frost is over by May 18 in 9 years out of 10, Tender plants like tomatoes wait longer than that, for warm soil: see the tomato question below.

    When is the first frost in Pocatello?

    Around October 11. It comes as early as September 26 in 1 year out of 10.

    When can I plant tomatoes in Pocatello?

    Set out tomato plants May 20 – Jun 17, after starting seeds indoors Mar 25 – Apr 8.

    When should I plant garlic in Pocatello?

    Plant garlic cloves Sep 25 – Oct 31, and harvest next summer when the lower leaves turn brown.

    How long is the growing season in Pocatello?

    About 162 frost-free days, shorter than most of the 86 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover.
