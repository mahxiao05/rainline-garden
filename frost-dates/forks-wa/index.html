Forks, WA frost dates and planting calendar

The typical last spring frost in Forks is April 20, and the first fall frost is around October 26. Sow peas from Mar 25; set out tomatoes from May 20.

Coast. Mild winters but cool, foggy summers. Frost is rarely the problem; warmth is. Pick early, cool-tolerant varieties of tomatoes and squash.

Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Quillayute Airport (56 m elevation). Gardens in cold hollows or at higher elevation can frost later in spring and earlier in fall.

Dates are a guide, not a guarantee. Check the soil before planting: cool-season crops need it workable and not soggy; warm-season crops need it warm. A $10 soil thermometer pays for itself.

Crop tips

Peas

Germinate in cool soil (about 40-50°F). Get them in early; they stall in summer heat.

Radishes

Sow a short row every two weeks for a steady supply.

Spinach

Bolts when days get long and warm, so spring sowings should go in early.

Lettuce

Tolerates light frost. Sow every two to three weeks; choose heat-tolerant types for later sowings.

Onions

Plant as soon as the soil is dry enough to work. Pick long-day varieties for the Northwest.

Carrots

Keep the seedbed moist until they sprout, which can take two to three weeks in cool soil.

Beets

Each seed cluster sprouts several seedlings; thin to 3 inches apart.

Kale

One of the best crops for Northwest winters; frost sweetens the leaves.

Broccoli

Harvest the main head while buds are tight; side shoots follow for weeks.

Cabbage

Even watering prevents split heads.

Swiss chard

Handles both cool spring weather and summer heat; harvest outer leaves for months.

Potatoes

Use certified seed potatoes. Hill soil around stems as they grow.

Bush beans

Seeds rot in cold, wet soil. Wait until soil is consistently above 60°F.

Sweet corn

Plant in blocks of at least four short rows, not one long row, for good pollination.

Cucumbers

Need warm soil and warm nights. Floating row cover helps in cool springs.

Zucchini and summer squash

Two plants feed most families. Pick fruit small and often.

Winter squash and pumpkins

Long season. Harvest before hard frost and cure in a warm spot for storage.

Tomatoes

OSU Extension advises waiting for soil consistently above 70°F. Early or cherry varieties ripen most reliably here.

Peppers

Even more heat-hungry than tomatoes. A warm wall or black plastic mulch helps.

Basil

Blackens below about 40°F, so it's the last thing to go out.

Garlic

Planted in fall, harvested the next summer when the lower leaves brown.
