    Vancouver, WA frost dates and planting calendar

    The typical last spring frost in Vancouver is March 29, and the first fall frost is around November 9. Plant peas from Mar 1; plant tomatoes from May 15.

    Last spring frostMarch 29Typical date. Frost is over by Apr 18 in 9 years out of 10.
    First fall frostNovember 9Typical date. Comes as early as Oct 22 in 1 year out of 10.
    Frost-free season225 daysYour growing season for frost-tender crops like tomatoes and squash.

    West-side valleys. The Willamette Valley, Puget Sound and southern Oregon valleys. Long frost-free seasons, but soil stays cold and wet into May, so warm crops wait for warm soil, not just the last frost.

    Frost dates are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Vancouver Pearson Field (9 m elevation).

    How the season in Vancouver compares

    With 225 frost-free days, Vancouver has the 19th-longest season of the 71 Oregon and Washington towns we cover. They run from 87 days in Burns to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, March 29, is the earliest of the towns we cover in Southwest Washington: 10 days earlier than Longview and Kelso and 24 days earlier than Centralia and Chehalis.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 20 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until April 18.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 6 and set out kale plants by August 20. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (November 9 on average) and to shortening days.
