    About Gresham

    Gresham sits at the west end of the Columbia River Gorge, east of Portland. Cold east winds blow out of the Gorge in winter, so Gresham and Troutdale often get sharper cold snaps and ice storms than central Portland.

    How the season in Gresham compares

    With 249 frost-free days, Gresham has the 10th-longest season of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, March 16, is in the middle of the towns we cover in the Portland area: 3 days later than Portland and 31 days earlier than Hillsboro and Beaverton.

    Late frosts are harder to predict here than in most towns. There are 27 days between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10, against 19 for a typical town on this site, so keep frost cover handy until April 12.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 6 and set out kale plants by August 20. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (November 20 on average) and to shortening days.

    Where these dates come from

    The frost dates for Gresham come from the NOAA weather station at Portland-Troutdale Airport, about 4 miles north of Gresham, at 30 feet. It's the closest weather station to Gresham with NOAA's 1991–2020 frost records. It's an airport station, out on open ground, so a garden in town, on a slope or in a low spot can run a few days earlier or later.

    Source: NOAA 1991–2020 climate normals. How we pick each town's station.

    Common questions about frost in Gresham
    When is the last frost in Gresham?

    March 16 in a typical year, and frost is over by April 12 in 9 years out of 10. Peas and other cool crops don't need to wait for it: peas can go in from March 1. Tomatoes, beans and squash wait longer than the frost date, until the soil warms (see the tomato question below).

    When is the first frost in Gresham?

    Around November 20 in a typical year, and as early as October 30 in 1 year out of 10. From about Oct 30 on, watch the forecast and pick or cover tomatoes, peppers, squash and basil before a cold night. Kale, carrots, leeks and cabbage handle a light frost fine and keep going.

    When can I plant tomatoes in Gresham?

    Set out tomato plants May 15 – Jun 12. That's about 9 weeks after the typical last frost, because tomatoes sit and sulk in cold soil until it reaches about 60°F. To grow your own from seed, start them indoors Mar 20 – Apr 3, or buy young plants at planting time.

    When should I plant garlic in Gresham?

    Plant garlic cloves Oct 1 – Nov 15, pointy end up, in a sunny bed. The cloves need a winter of cold to split into a full bulb, which is why fall planting works and spring planting often gives one small round bulb. Dig the next summer, once the lower leaves have turned brown.

    What growing zone is Gresham in?

    USDA hardiness zone 8b–9a, depending on your ZIP code, on the 2023 USDA map: the coldest winter night is usually 15 to 25°F. The zone tells you which perennials, shrubs and trees survive the winter. For vegetables it matters much less: go by the frost dates and planting calendar on this page instead.

    How long is the growing season in Gresham?

    About 249 frost-free days, from March 16 to November 20 in a typical year. That's one of the longest of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. 21 plants on the calendar get a second planting for fall here, including radishes, spinach and lettuce.

    How much rain does Gresham get?

    About 41 inches a year, counting melted snow. December is the wettest month and July the driest. Only 3.3 inches (8% of the year) falls from June to August, so plan to water through summer even though the year is wet overall. Figures are NOAA 1991–2020 normals.
