    About McMinnville and Newberg

    McMinnville and Newberg are in Yamhill County, the center of Oregon's wine country. Pinot noir grows on the red hillside soils of the Dundee Hills, and hazelnut orchards fill much of the valley floor.

    Gardening in the Willamette Valley. Valley-floor soils are often heavy clay that stays wet into spring, so raised beds help early crops. Slugs thrive in the long rainy season, and the summers are dry: water deeply from July until the fall rains return.

    Frost date isn't planting date. The typical last frost in McMinnville and Newberg is April 15, but tomatoes wait until May 15, about 4 weeks later, because the soil is still too cold for them to grow. Why soil temperature matters.

    How the season in McMinnville and Newberg compares

    With 194 frost-free days, McMinnville and Newberg have the 38th-longest season of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover. They run from 72 days in McCall to 330 days in Brookings and Gold Beach.

    The typical last frost here, April 15, is in the middle of the towns we cover in the Willamette Valley: 12 days later than Salem and 9 days earlier than Cottage Grove.

    The gap between the typical last frost and the date it's over in 9 years out of 10 is 21 days, about average for the towns on this site. Frost after the typical date still happens about half the time, so plan on covering warm crops until May 6.

    The season is long enough for every warm crop on our list, even pumpkins and sweet potatoes, to finish from a normal planting date.

    For a fall and winter garden, sow carrots by August 6 and set out kale plants by August 20. Fall crops here are timed to the first frost (October 26 on average) and to shortening days.

    Where these dates come from

    The frost dates for McMinnville and Newberg come from the NOAA weather station at McMinnville Municipal Airport, about 3 miles east of McMinnville and about 11 miles southwest of Newberg, at 160 feet. It's an airport station, out on open ground, so a garden in town, on a slope or in a low spot can run a few days earlier or later.

    Source: NOAA 1991–2020 climate normals. How we pick each town's station.

    Common questions about frost in McMinnville and Newberg

    When is the last frost in McMinnville and Newberg?

    April 15 in a typical year. Frost is over by May 6 in 9 years out of 10. Tender plants like tomatoes wait longer than that, for warm soil: see the tomato question below.

    When is the first frost in McMinnville and Newberg?

    Around October 26. It comes as early as October 5 in 1 year out of 10.

    When can I plant tomatoes in McMinnville and Newberg?

    Set out tomato plants May 15 – Jun 12, after starting seeds indoors Mar 20 – Apr 3.

    When should I plant garlic in McMinnville and Newberg?

    Plant garlic cloves Oct 1 – Nov 15, and harvest next summer when the lower leaves turn brown.

    What growing zone is McMinnville and Newberg in?

    USDA hardiness zone 8b, on the 2023 USDA map: the coldest winter night is usually 15 to 20°F. The zone tells you which perennials, shrubs and trees survive the winter; for vegetables, go by the frost dates and planting calendar instead.

    How long is the growing season in McMinnville and Newberg?

    About 194 frost-free days, longer than most of the 116 Oregon, Washington and Idaho towns we cover.

    How much rain does McMinnville and Newberg get?

    About 38 inches a year, counting melted snow. December is the wettest month and July the driest, with 1.9 inches from June to August (NOAA 1991–2020 normals).
