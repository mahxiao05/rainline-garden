      How to use these dates

      "Typical" means the median: in half of years the last spring frost comes later. Each town page also shows the date when frost is over in 9 years out of 10. Tender plants like tomatoes and basil need that, and warm soil too, so they often go out weeks later: each town page gives their dates.

      Each town's dates come from one weather station, usually an airport. Gardens in cold hollows or at higher elevation can frost later in spring and earlier in fall; city gardens and warm, south-facing spots often run a week or so ahead. If your garden is in a frost pocket, add a week to spring dates and take a week off fall ones.

      Dates are a guide, not a guarantee. Check the soil before planting: cool-season crops need it workable and not soggy, and warm-season crops need it warm. A $10 soil thermometer pays for itself.
