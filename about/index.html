About Rainline Garden

A free planting calendar built for Pacific Northwest gardens, not adapted from a national chart.

National planting guides treat the whole country the same way. The Northwest doesn't cooperate: coastal gardens rarely frost but never get hot, west-side valleys have long seasons and cold spring soil, and the high desert can frost in July. Rainline Garden gives each area its own dates, built from NOAA weather records and Oregon State University Extension guidance.

Jigmey Dorjee grew up in Oregon, where gardening means working with the weather: cold, wet springs, dry summers and long, mild falls. He's an experienced home gardener who has grown his own vegetables for years, and he knows firsthand that a Northwest garden runs on a different calendar than the national planting charts. He writes Rainline Garden's articles and checks the advice against NOAA weather records and Oregon State University Extension guidance, so what you read combines hands-on growing with published research.

How we make this site

  • Frost dates come from NOAA's 1991–2020 climate normals for the weather station nearest each town.
  • Planting windows follow Oregon State University Extension guidance for each crop, counted from those frost dates.
  • Articles combine that research with hands-on growing experience. Each one is checked against the sources before it's published and updated when the guidance changes.
  • Corrections are welcome. If a date doesn't match your garden, tell us and we'll check it.
