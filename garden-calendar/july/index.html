    What to plant in July in Oregon and Washington

    Peak summer and the hinge of the year: harvest summer crops while you sow and plant for fall and winter. Up to 42 of our plants can go in somewhere in the Northwest this month.

    What to plant in July, by region

    West-side valleys (Portland): plant carrots, broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower and radicchio outside.

    Coast (Astoria): plant carrots, broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower and radicchio outside.

    Columbia Basin and eastern valleys (Pendleton): plant carrots, broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower and radicchio outside.

    High desert (Bend and Redmond): plant spinach, lettuce, carrots, beets, Swiss chard, bush beans, sweet corn, cucumbers, zucchini and summer squash, tomatoes, peppers, basil, dahlias, cilantro, dill, arugula, bok choy, turnips, kohlrabi, eggplant, melons, pole beans, chives, mint, thyme, oregano, sunflowers, fig trees, lavender, tomatillos, zinnias, celery, ground cherries, sweet potatoes, mustard greens, mâche, sage, lemon balm, tarragon, cosmos, nasturtiums and marigolds outside.

    Inland Northwest (Spokane): plant carrots, broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower and radicchio outside.

    July garden jobs

    Plant and sow

    • Sow fall carrots, beets, turnips and kohlrabi. West side
    • Set out fall broccoli, cabbage and kale starts late in the month. West side
    • Sow fall peas and bush beans early in the month. West side
    • Sow lettuce in a spot with afternoon shade so it doesn't bolt.

    Start indoors

    • Start more brassicas for overwintering west of the Cascades: purple sprouting broccoli, winter cabbage. West side

    Harvest

    • Garlic when the lower third of the leaves turn brown. Cure it somewhere shady and airy.
    • Zucchini every day or two, before they turn into marrows.
    • Beans, cucumbers, early tomatoes and raspberries.
    • Onions once the tops flop over. East side

    Garden care

    • Water consistently. Uneven watering causes blossom-end rot on tomatoes and splits in cabbages.
    • Mulch thickly to keep roots cool.
    • Shade new seedlings and transplants during heat waves.

    Pests and problems

    • Watch for powdery mildew on squash and cucumbers; improve airflow and water at the base.
    • Check tomatoes for late blight after any cool, wet spell. West side
    • Spider mites show up on stressed plants in hot, dry weather. East side

    Fruit, berries and flowers

    • Cut summer raspberry canes that finished fruiting down to the ground.
    • Order garlic seed now for fall planting; good varieties sell out.
    • Keep picking flowers; dahlias bloom more the more you cut.
