    What to plant in January in Oregon and Washington

    The quiet month. Wet and cold on the west side, frozen east of the Cascades. Good for planning, ordering and pruning.

    What to plant in January, by region

    West-side valleys (Portland): nothing goes outside yet, but you can start lettuce, broccoli, cabbage, parsley, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, leeks, bok choy, chives and snapdragons indoors.

    Coast (Astoria): nothing goes outside yet, but you can start leeks and snapdragons indoors.

    East-side valleys (Pendleton): nothing goes outside yet, but you can start leeks and snapdragons indoors.

    High desert (Bend and Redmond): nothing goes in this month.

    Inland Northwest (Spokane): nothing goes in this month.

    January garden jobs

    Plant and sow

    • Nothing goes in the ground yet. Leave wet soil alone so it keeps its structure.

    Start indoors

    • Late in the month, start onions and leeks from seed under lights if you want big bulbs. West-side valleys

    Harvest

    • Pick kale, leeks, collards and overwintered carrots as needed. West-side valleys
    • Use stored squash, potatoes and onions, oldest first.

    Garden care

    • Order seeds while catalogs have full stock. Pick short-season and cool-summer varieties.
    • Sketch this year's beds and rotate crop families so none sits in the same spot two years running.
    • Check stored squash, potatoes and onions, and remove any that are going soft.
    • Check seed packets you already have. Most stay good two to three years if kept cool and dry.

    Pests and problems

    • Look over overwintering brassicas for aphid colonies tucked into the leaves. West-side valleys

    Fruit, berries and flowers

    • Prune dormant apple and pear trees on dry days between rains.
    • Prune blueberries: take out a few of the oldest canes at the base.
    • Cut fall-bearing raspberry canes to the ground.
