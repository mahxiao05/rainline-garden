    February jobs

    Plant and sow

    • Plant bare-root fruit trees, berries and rhubarb whenever the soil isn't waterlogged. West-side valleys
    • Plant onion sets and shallots late in the month in raised beds that drain well. West-side valleys

    Start indoors

    • Start onions and leeks indoors if you didn't in January.
    • Start broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower and lettuce indoors late in the month. West-side valleys

    Harvest

    • Keep picking hardy greens and leeks. West-side valleys
    • Purple sprouting broccoli planted last summer starts sending up shoots. West-side valleys

    Garden care

    • Clean and sharpen tools, and wash old pots and seed trays in soapy water.
    • Stay off wet soil. If a squeezed handful drips or stays in a tight ball, it's too wet to dig.
    • Set up a seed-starting shelf with a shop light hung a few inches above the trays.
    • Turn under or cut down winter cover crops a few weeks before you plan to plant those beds. West-side valleys

    Pests and problems

    • Put out slug traps around early greens and emerging bulbs on mild nights. West-side valleys

    Fruit, berries and flowers

    • Finish pruning fruit trees before buds break.
    • Prune summer-flowering shrubs and roses late in the month. West-side valleys
    • Spread compost around rhubarb and asparagus beds.

    February jobs for tricky crops

    Steps from our deeper guides that fall in February. Each links to the full list for that plant.

    • Dahlias February or March (optional): Start a tuber early in a 1-gallon pot of fresh potting soil, with plenty of light so it doesn't get leggy.
    • Peach trees Late February: One more leaf curl spray. Dormant oil for aphid and mite eggs and scale goes on separately, not mixed in.
    • Fig trees January or February: Prune while dormant to open up the middle and keep the tree under about 8 feet.
    • Grapes January to early March: Prune while dormant. Expect to cut off about 90% of last year's growth, leaving 20 to 60 buds per vine.
    • Raspberries December to February: Summer-bearing kinds: remove weak, broken or damaged canes, keep the row 6 to 12 inches wide, and cut the rest to about 6 feet.
