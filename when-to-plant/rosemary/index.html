    How to grow rosemary

    1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
    2. Plant young rosemary plants on your city's dates, 24 to 36 inches apart. Plant at the depth it grew in the nursery pot.
    3. Water until established, then only in dry spells. In cold eastern areas, grow it in a pot and move it to a cool, bright spot for winter.
    4. Harvest: Snip sprig tips as needed. Don't cut back into bare wood; it won't regrow.

    Varieties that do well here

    • Arp (selected for cold hardiness)
    • Hill Hardy
    • Tuscan Blue

    Watch out for

    Wet winter soil kills more rosemary than cold does. Plant in fast-draining soil or a raised bed.

    How it works

    The plant biology behind the advice above, in plain language.

    Wet soil kills more rosemary than cold

    Rosemary comes from dry, rocky hillsides around the Mediterranean. Its roots rot when they sit in cold, soggy soil, which is why more rosemary dies in a wet Northwest winter than in a cold, dry one. Plant it in fast-draining soil, on a slope or in a raised bed, and never in a spot where water pools. Mixing gravel into heavy clay helps.

    Cold hardiness

    Most rosemary varieties are damaged somewhere around 15 to 20°F. Hardier selections like Arp and Madeline Hill (also sold as Hill Hardy) survive colder winters, especially in well-drained soil. Cold, drying winds make damage worse, so a spot near a south-facing wall is ideal. In colder eastern areas, grow rosemary in a pot and move it to a cool, bright spot for winter.

    Soil and feeding

    Rosemary grows best in lean, well-drained soil and tolerates neutral to slightly alkaline pH. It doesn't need fertilizer. Rich soil and extra water make it grow fast and soft, with less of the aromatic oil that gives it flavor. Once established, water only during long dry spells.

    Pruning

    Rosemary has very few dormant buds on old, bare wood, so cuts into leafless stems usually don't regrow. Prune lightly, within the green, leafy part of each stem, right after flowering in spring. Regular light trims keep it compact and stop it from getting leggy.

    In containers

    Use a terracotta pot, which dries out faster, and a gritty mix like cactus potting soil. Water when the top inch or two is dry. Indoors in winter, rosemary needs a cool, very bright spot; warm rooms and low light lead to powdery mildew and leaf drop.

    What you'll need

    • Terracotta pot
    • Cactus and citrus potting mix

    Common questions about growing rosemary

    Why did my rosemary die over winter?

    Most often from wet, cold soil rather than cold alone. The roots rot in waterlogged ground. Replant in fast-draining soil or a raised bed, and choose a hardier variety like Arp.

    Can I leave potted rosemary outside in winter?

    West of the Cascades, usually yes, if the pot drains well and sits against a sheltered wall. In colder areas, roots in pots freeze more easily than in the ground, so move the pot into an unheated garage or other cool, bright spot during hard freezes.

    How do I fix leggy rosemary?

    Prune lightly within the green growth after flowering, and repeat every year. Cuts into old, bare wood rarely regrow, so very woody plants are often best replaced.
