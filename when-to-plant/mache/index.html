      How to grow mâche

      1. Prepare the bed: loosen the soil and mix in an inch or two of compost.
      2. Plant seeds on your city's dates, ¼ to ½ inch deep, Thin to 3 to 4 inches apart.
      3. Keep the soil evenly moist until sprouts appear, which can take one to three weeks in cool soil.
      4. Plant again on your city's dates for a fall harvest.
      5. Harvest: Cut whole rosettes at the soil line once they have 8 to 10 leaves, or pick the larger ones and leave the rest to grow. Rinse well, since grit hides in the rosettes.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      A green for the dark months

      Mâche, also called corn salad or lamb's lettuce, makes small rosettes of mild, nutty leaves. It's one of the most cold-hardy salad greens there is, which is why Northwest gardeners treat it as a winter crop rather than a spring one.

      The main sowing goes in from mid-August into September. Plants grow slowly through fall and can be picked through most west-side winters. Spring sowings work too, but they bolt as soon as days lengthen.

      Getting it to sprout

      The seed germinates best in cool soil and poorly in summer heat, so late-summer sowings can be slow and patchy. Keep the seedbed moist and lightly shaded until seedlings appear, which can take one to two weeks.

      It isn't fussy about soil. Plants are small, so sow in a band and thin to 3 or 4 inches apart. An empty bed after summer crops, a cold frame or a spot between garlic rows all work.

      Harvest and care

      Cut whole rosettes at the soil line, or pick the biggest plants and let the others keep growing. Rinse carefully, since soil splashes into the rosettes in winter rain.

      Slugs are the main pest. A cold frame or row cover speeds growth in winter and keeps leaves cleaner. In spring, let a few plants flower and drop seed, and mâche often comes back on its own in fall.

      Good neighbors and crop rotation

      Good neighbors

      • Garlic. Fills the bare winter soil between rows.
      • Kale and leeks. Other winter crops that share a bed through the cold months.

      Valerian group of the honeysuckle family: Mâche has no close relatives among common vegetables, so it fits anywhere in a rotation. It's a handy winter crop for beds cleared of summer vegetables.

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with mâche in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Slugs

      Signs: Holes in rosettes in winter.

      What to do: Slug bait on mild, wet nights.

      Poor sprouting

      Signs: Patchy rows in warm soil.

      What to do: Sow once late-summer soil cools, and keep the seedbed moist.

      Common questions about growing mâche

      When should I plant mâche in the Northwest?

      Sow in late summer, from mid-August into September, for picking from late fall through winter. You can also sow in early spring, but it bolts quickly as days lengthen.

      Why isn't my mâche sprouting?

      The soil is probably too warm. Mâche germinates best in cool soil. Sow a little later, keep the bed moist and shaded, and be patient for up to two weeks.

      What does mâche taste like?

      Mild, tender and slightly nutty, with no bitterness. It's good on its own with a light dressing or mixed with sharper greens like arugula.
