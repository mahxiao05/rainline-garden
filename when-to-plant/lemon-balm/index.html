    How to grow lemon balm

    1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
    2. Plant young lemon balm plants on your city's dates, 18 to 24 inches apart. Plant at the depth it grew in the nursery pot.
    3. It tolerates most soils and some shade. Cut the whole plant back by half in midsummer to keep it bushy and stop it seeding.
    4. Harvest: Cut stems before flowering for the strongest lemon scent. Use leaves fresh in tea, or dry them quickly in the shade, since they lose scent slowly in damp air.

    Varieties that do well here

    • Common lemon balm
    • Aurea (gold-variegated)
    • Lime balm

    Watch out for

    It seeds itself all over the garden. Shear the plants back before the small white flowers set seed, which also brings a fresh crop of leaves.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    An easy herb for tea

    Lemon balm is a hardy perennial in the mint family, grown for its lemon-scented leaves. It grows in almost any soil and takes part shade better than most herbs, which makes it useful in shady Northwest yards.

    It dies back in winter and regrows from the crown each spring. Unlike mint, it doesn't spread by long runners, but it seeds itself freely.

    Keeping it tidy

    Cut plants back by about half before the small white flowers set seed. That stops seedlings and brings a fresh crop of tender leaves. A second cut in late summer keeps plants neat.

    In full sun it needs some summer water; in part shade it gets by with less. Divide crowded clumps every few years in spring.

    Harvest and use

    Pick stems before flowering, when the scent is strongest. Use the leaves fresh in tea, salads and desserts.

    Dried lemon balm loses scent fast. Dry it quickly in a warm, dark, airy place and store it airtight, or freeze leaves in ice cubes. Bees love the flowers if you let a few bloom.

    Good neighbors

    Good neighbors

    • Fruit trees and berries. Its small flowers draw bees.

    Keep apart

    • Tidy beds. It seeds itself freely; keep it where a few strays won't matter.

    Pests and problems

    What most often goes wrong with lemon balm in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

    Self-seeding

    Signs: Seedlings all over the garden.

    What to do: Shear plants back before the flowers set seed.

    Powdery mildew

    Signs: White coating on leaves in late summer.

    What to do: Cut back hard for a flush of clean leaves.

    Common questions about growing lemon balm

    Is lemon balm invasive?

    It doesn't run like mint, but it seeds itself widely. Cutting it back before it flowers keeps it under control.

    Does lemon balm grow in shade?

    Yes. It grows well in part shade, and the leaves are often more tender there. In deep shade it gets leggy.

    Why doesn't my dried lemon balm smell like lemon?

    The scent fades during slow drying and storage. Dry it quickly, or use it fresh or frozen.
