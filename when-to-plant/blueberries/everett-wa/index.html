When to plant blueberries in Everett, WA

Plant blueberry bushes in Everett Mar 15 – May 3.

Easy Plant potted or bare-root bushes

Why these dates

Everett's typical last spring frost is March 9. In the west-side valleys, the soil is usually ready for blueberries around March 15.

West-side valleys. The Willamette Valley, Puget Sound and southern Oregon valleys. Long frost-free seasons, but soil stays cold and wet into May, so warm crops wait for warm soil, not just the last frost.

Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun
Spacing
4 to 5 feet apart
Planting depth
Same depth as in the pot
Frost
Hardy perennial; comes back every year
First harvest
Small crops in year two or three; full crops by year five

Step by step

  1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
  2. Plant blueberry bushes Mar 15 – May 3, 4 to 5 feet apart. Same depth as in the pot.
  3. Mix peat moss or acidic compost into the planting hole, mulch with sawdust or bark, and pinch off flowers the first year.
  4. Harvest: Pick berries a few days after they turn fully blue, when they roll off into your hand.

Varieties that do well here

  • Duke (early)
  • Chandler (huge berries)
  • Bluecrop
  • Top Hat (for containers)

Watch out for

Birds. Cover bushes with netting as berries color, or plant extra.

Need acidic soil (pH 4.5 to 5.5), which much of western Oregon and Washington already has. Plant two varieties for better crops. West of the Cascades, fall planting works well too.

What you'll need

  • Blueberry bushes
  • Fertilizer for acid-loving plants
  • Bird netting

Common questions

When is the last frost in Everett, WA?

Typically March 9. In 9 years out of 10, frost is over by March 29. These are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Everett Paine Field.

Is it too late to plant blueberries in Everett?

The spring planting window runs through May 3. Potted plants can still go in later if you water them faithfully through their first summer. Fall planting also works well here.

How long until I can pick blueberries?

Small crops in year two or three; full crops by year five. Pick berries a few days after they turn fully blue, when they roll off into your hand.
