When to plant blueberries in Bend and Redmond, OR

Plant blueberry bushes in Bend and Redmond May 12 – Jun 30.

Easy Plant potted or bare-root bushes

Why these dates

Bend and Redmond's typical last spring frost is June 9. As a hardy perennial, this crop can go in about 4 weeks before that.

High desert and mountains. Central and high eastern Oregon. Frost is possible almost any month. Use row covers, wall-o-water or cloches for warm crops, and choose short-season varieties.

Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun
Spacing
4 to 5 feet apart
Planting depth
Same depth as in the pot
Frost
Hardy perennial; comes back every year
First harvest
Small crops in year two or three; full crops by year five

Step by step

  1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
  2. Plant blueberry bushes May 12 – Jun 30, 4 to 5 feet apart. Same depth as in the pot.
  3. Mix peat moss or acidic compost into the planting hole, mulch with sawdust or bark, and pinch off flowers the first year.
  4. Harvest: Pick berries a few days after they turn fully blue, when they roll off into your hand.

Varieties that do well here

  • Duke (early)
  • Chandler (huge berries)
  • Bluecrop
  • Top Hat (for containers)

Watch out for

Birds. Cover bushes with netting as berries color, or plant extra.

Need acidic soil (pH 4.5 to 5.5), which much of western Oregon and Washington already has. Plant two varieties for better crops. West of the Cascades, fall planting works well too.

What you'll need

  • Blueberry bushes
  • Fertilizer for acid-loving plants
  • Bird netting

Common questions

When is the last frost in Bend and Redmond, OR?

Typically June 9. In 9 years out of 10, frost is over by June 26. These are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Redmond Roberts Field.

Is it too late to plant blueberries in Bend and Redmond?

The spring planting window runs through June 30. Potted plants can still go in later if you water them faithfully through their first summer.

How long until I can pick blueberries?

Small crops in year two or three; full crops by year five. Pick berries a few days after they turn fully blue, when they roll off into your hand.
