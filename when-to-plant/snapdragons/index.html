      How to grow snapdragons

      1. Start seeds indoors on your city's dates in trays or small pots under a grow light or in your sunniest window. Or skip this step and buy young plants (starts) at a garden center.
      2. Harden off the young plants: about a week before planting, set them outside for a few hours a day, a little longer each day.
      3. Plant them in the garden on your city's dates, 9 to 12 inches apart. Press seeds onto the soil surface; they need light to sprout.
      4. Water deeply once or twice a week rather than a little every day, and mulch once the soil is warm.
      5. Harvest: Cut stems when the bottom third of the flowers are open. Pinch young plants once for more, shorter stems, or leave them for one tall spike.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      Starting from seed

      Snapdragon seeds are tiny and need light to germinate, so press them onto the surface of moist seed-starting mix without covering them. They take one to two weeks to sprout and grow slowly at first.

      Start them indoors eight to ten weeks before your last frost, or buy young plants. Harden them off and plant out a few weeks before the last frost; they handle light frost and grow best in cool weather.

      Growing tall stems

      Give them full sun and good soil with compost. Pinch young plants above the third set of leaves for more, shorter stems, or leave them unpinched for a single tall spike.

      Tall types like Rocket need support. Stretch netting over the bed early so stems grow up through it.

      Cutting and rust

      Cut stems when the lower third of the flowers are open. Cut low, and side shoots bloom again later.

      Rust causes orange-brown spots under the leaves in damp weather. Water at the soil, give plants space, and pull and bin infected plants. On the west side, snapdragons often survive mild winters and bloom early the next year.

      Common questions about growing snapdragons

      When should I plant snapdragons in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 15 – Apr 26 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 15 – May 27 in Spokane, Apr 1 – May 13 in Boise and May 12 – Jun 23 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      Do snapdragons come back every year?

      They're short-lived perennials grown as annuals. In mild west-side winters many survive and bloom again early the next year, though second-year plants are often weaker.

      Why won't my snapdragon seeds sprout?

      They were probably covered. The seeds need light, so press them onto the surface and keep them moist.

      Can snapdragons handle frost?

      Yes, light frost. Plant them out a few weeks before your last frost, and cover them on very cold nights.
