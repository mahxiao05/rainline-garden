    When to plant daffodils in Bellingham, WA

    Plant daffodil bulbs in Bellingham from Oct 1 – Nov 15. They root over winter and bloom the next spring.

    Why these dates

    Daffodils need winter chill to bloom, so they go in during fall once the soil has cooled, ideally a few weeks before it freezes. In Bellingham the first fall frost usually comes around October 30.

    West-side valleys. The Willamette Valley, Puget Sound and southern Oregon valleys. Long frost-free seasons, but soil stays cold and wet into May, so warm crops wait for warm soil, not just the last frost.

    How to grow daffodils in Bellingham

    1. Buy firm bulbs from a garden center or bulb grower.
    2. Pick a spot with soil that drains well; full sun or light shade both work.
    3. Plant them Oct 1 – Nov 15, 6 inches deep, pointed end up, 4 to 6 inches apart.
    4. Water once after planting. After blooming, leave the leaves until they yellow so the bulbs come back next year.
    5. Harvest: Cut when buds are just opening. Their sap harms other flowers in a vase, so stand them alone for a few hours first. Let the leaves die back on their own.

    Varieties that do well here

    • Tête-à-Tête (early, small)
    • Dutch Master
    • Thalia (white)

    Watch out for

    Bulbs rot in soggy soil. Plant on a slope or in well-drained beds.

    Deer and rodents leave them alone, and they come back for decades. Plant in fall once the soil cools.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    Why daffodils are so easy

    Daffodil bulbs, leaves and flowers contain toxic compounds, so deer, voles and squirrels leave them alone. That makes them the go-to spring bulb where animals eat tulips. They need a winter chill to bloom, which the whole Northwest provides.

    Their one weakness is soggy soil. Bulbs sitting in cold, waterlogged ground over a wet west-side winter can rot. Plant on a slope, in a raised bed or anywhere water doesn't puddle after rain.

    Let the leaves work

    After flowering, the leaves keep making food and sending it down to the bulb for next year's bloom. Leave them in place until they yellow, usually about six weeks. Cutting them early, tying them in knots or braiding them shades and weakens the leaves and leads to fewer flowers.

    A sprinkle of bulb fertilizer or compost as shoots come up in late winter helps older plantings. Snap off spent flowers so the plant doesn't spend energy on seed.

    Dividing and problems

    Daffodil bulbs multiply, and after several years clumps can get so crowded that they produce lots of leaves but few flowers. When that happens, dig the clump once the leaves have died back in early summer, separate the bulbs and replant them right away or in fall.

    Narcissus bulb fly larvae can hollow out bulbs, leaving soft, empty ones. Firm the soil around the leaves as they fade so the flies can't reach the bulbs, and discard any soft bulbs you dig up.

    Common questions

    When is the last frost in Bellingham, WA?

    Typically March 31. In 9 years out of 10, frost is over by April 18. These are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Bellingham International Airport.

    When do daffodils bloom in Bellingham?

    From late winter into spring, depending on the variety and the year. West-side gardens see the first ones in February or March. Cut when buds are just opening. Their sap harms other flowers in a vase, so stand them alone for a few hours first. Let the leaves die back on their own.

    When do daffodils bloom?

    Blooms from late winter to spring, depending on variety. Cut when buds are just opening. Their sap harms other flowers in a vase, so stand them alone for a few hours first. Let the leaves die back on their own.

    Why didn't my daffodils bloom this year?

    Common reasons are leaves cut off too early last year, crowded clumps, too much shade or bulbs planted too shallow. Let the leaves die back on their own, divide crowded clumps and make sure they get some spring sun.

    Can I cut back daffodil leaves after flowering?

    Wait until they turn yellow, usually about six weeks after bloom. Until then they're feeding the bulb for next year. Plant them among perennials whose new growth hides the fading leaves.

    Can I plant daffodil bulbs in spring?

    Bulbs sold in fall should go in by late fall or early winter. If you find unplanted bulbs in spring, plant them anyway; they may bloom late or skip a year, but firm bulbs often recover.
