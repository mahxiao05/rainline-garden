    When to plant daffodils in Wenatchee, WA

    Plant daffodil bulbs in Wenatchee from Oct 1 – Nov 1. They root over winter and bloom the next spring.

    Easy Plant bulbs

    Why these dates

    Daffodils need winter chill to bloom, so they go in during fall once the soil has cooled, ideally a few weeks before it freezes. In Wenatchee the first fall frost usually comes around October 24.

    Columbia Basin and low eastern valleys. Hot, dry summers and quick spring warm-up. Warm crops go in earlier than west of the Cascades, and irrigation matters more than rain.

    Difficulty
    Easy
    Sun
    Full sun to part shade
    Spacing
    4 to 6 inches apart
    Planting depth
    6 inches deep, pointed end up
    Frost
    Fully hardy; needs winter cold to bloom
    Flowers
    Blooms from late winter to spring, depending on variety

    Step by step

    1. Buy firm bulbs from a garden center or bulb grower.
    2. Pick a spot with soil that drains well; full sun or light shade both work.
    3. Plant them Oct 1 – Nov 1, 6 inches deep, pointed end up, 4 to 6 inches apart.
    4. Water once after planting. After blooming, leave the leaves until they yellow so the bulbs come back next year.
    5. Harvest: Cut when buds are just opening. Their sap harms other flowers in a vase, so stand them alone for a few hours first. Let the leaves die back on their own.

    Varieties that do well here

    • Tête-à-Tête (early, small)
    • Dutch Master
    • Thalia (white)

    Watch out for

    Bulbs rot in soggy soil. Plant on a slope or in well-drained beds.

    Deer and rodents leave them alone, and they come back for decades. Plant in fall once the soil cools.

    What you'll need

    • Daffodil bulbs
    • Bulb planter tool

    Common questions

    When is the last frost in Wenatchee, WA?

    Typically April 8. In 9 years out of 10, frost is over by April 23. These are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Wenatchee Pangborn Airport.

    When do daffodils bloom in Wenatchee?

    From late winter into spring, depending on the variety and the year. West-side gardens see the first ones in February or March. Cut when buds are just opening. Their sap harms other flowers in a vase, so stand them alone for a few hours first. Let the leaves die back on their own.
