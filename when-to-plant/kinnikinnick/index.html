    How to grow kinnikinnick

    1. Pick a sunny slope, bank or bed with sandy or gravelly soil that drains fast. Don't add compost; it prefers lean soil.
    2. Plant potted kinnikinnick on your city's dates, 12 to 24 inches apart. Same depth as in the pot; don't bury the stems.
    3. Water through the first summer, then only in long droughts. Weed carefully until the mat closes.
    4. Harvest: Enjoy the berries as winter bird food. They're edible but dry and mealy, so most people leave them.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    A tough evergreen groundcover

    Kinnikinnick, also called bearberry, is a low, trailing evergreen shrub with small, glossy leaves. It has pink, bell-shaped flowers in spring and red berries in fall and winter that feed birds.

    It grows wild on sandy, rocky ground on both sides of the Cascades, from coastal bluffs to mountain slopes, and it's an early nectar source for native bees.

    Planting for success

    Give it sun or light shade and soil that drains fast: sandy, gravelly or rocky. It fails in heavy clay and wet soil. Don't add compost or fertilizer.

    It grows slowly. Plant small plants 12 to 24 inches apart for coverage in a few years, and mulch between them with gravel or bark to hold back weeds. Don't bury the stems.

    Care

    Water through the first summer, then only during long droughts. Once established it needs almost nothing.

    Vancouver Jade is a vigorous selection with good disease resistance. Massachusetts is another widely sold, hardy selection.

    Good neighbors

    Pests and problems

    What most often goes wrong with kinnikinnick in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

    Root rot

    Signs: Plants brown and die in heavy, wet soil.

    What to do: Plant in sandy or gravelly soil, or on a slope.

    Slow fill

    Signs: Weeds grow in the gaps.

    What to do: Mulch with gravel or bark between plants.

    Leaf spot

    Signs: Dark spots on the leaves in wet weather.

    What to do: Usually minor; improve airflow.

    Common questions about growing kinnikinnick

    Is kinnikinnick the same as bearberry?

    Yes. Both are common names for Arctostaphylos uva-ursi.

    How fast does kinnikinnick spread?

    Slowly at first. Expect two to four years to fill in when plants are a foot or two apart.

    Can you eat kinnikinnick berries?

    They're edible but dry and mealy. Most gardeners leave them for the birds.
