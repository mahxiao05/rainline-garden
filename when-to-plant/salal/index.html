      How to grow salal

      1. Pick a spot in part shade, such as under tall trees, with acidic soil, and mix in leaf mold or bark compost.
      2. Plant potted salal on your city's dates, 2 to 3 feet apart. Same depth as in the pot or nursery.
      3. Water through the first two summers and mulch with leaves or bark. No fertilizer needed.
      4. Harvest: Pick the dark purple berries in late summer when they're soft. They're mild and seedy; mix them with other berries for jam.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      The forest-floor evergreen

      Salal is the glossy, leathery evergreen shrub that covers the forest floor in western Oregon, western Washington and along the coast. It has pink, bell-shaped flowers in late spring and dark purple berries in late summer.

      Its height depends on the spot: knee-high in dry shade, head-high in rich, moist woods. Florists use the cut foliage, which lasts a long time in arrangements.

      Planting

      Plant in part shade in acidic soil rich in organic matter, such as under conifers. Mix in leaf mold or bark compost. On the coast it handles full sun; inland, full sun and dry soil stress it.

      Salal is famously slow to settle in. Expect a year or two with little top growth while roots establish. After that, it spreads by underground stems into a dense groundcover.

      Berries and care

      The berries are edible, mild and seedy. People eat them fresh, dried or in jam, often mixed with other fruit. Birds and other wildlife eat them too.

      Water through the first two summers and mulch with leaves or bark. Once established, it needs no fertilizer or irrigation in shade.

      Good neighbors

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with salal in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Slow start

      Signs: Plants sit still for a year or two.

      What to do: Mulch and water deeply through the first summers.

      Yellow leaves

      Signs: Pale leaves in heavy or alkaline soil.

      What to do: Add bark mulch and avoid lime.

      Common questions about growing salal

      Are salal berries edible?

      Yes. They're mild and seedy, good in jam or mixed with other berries.

      Why isn't my salal growing?

      It's slow to establish and can sit still for a year or two. Make sure the soil is acidic and the plant isn't in hot, dry sun inland.

      Does salal grow east of the Cascades?

      Rarely. It's a plant of the wet west side and coast and struggles in hot, dry inland summers and alkaline soil.
