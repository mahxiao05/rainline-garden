    How to grow sunflowers

    1. Prepare the bed: loosen the soil and mix in an inch or two of compost.
    2. Plant seeds on your city's dates, 1 inch deep, 6 inches (small types) to 24 inches (giants) apart.
    3. Keep the soil evenly moist until sprouts appear, which can take one to three weeks in cool soil.
    4. Harvest: Cut flowers when the petals are just opening. For seeds, let heads dry on the plant until the back turns brown, and cover them from birds.

    Varieties that do well here

    • Mammoth Grey Stripe (giant)
    • ProCut (for cutting)
    • Teddy Bear (dwarf)

    Watch out for

    Birds and slugs eat seedlings. Cover the seedbed until plants are a few inches tall.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    Timing and sowing

    Sunflowers sprout fastest in warm soil, so sow after the soil has warmed in late spring. In cold, wet soil, seeds sit for days and seedlings get eaten before they grow. Sow directly where they'll grow, since their long taproot makes transplanting hard.

    For a steady supply of cut flowers, sow a short row every two weeks into early July. Single-stem cutting varieties like ProCut bloom once, then they're done, so succession sowing keeps flowers coming. Branching types bloom over a longer stretch.

    Wind, soil and support

    Sunflowers aren't fussy about soil as long as it drains, but giants like Mammoth Grey Stripe need extra compost and steady water to reach full height. They also catch a lot of wind. Plant tall varieties in a sheltered spot, mound soil around the base of the stems as they grow, and stake them before late-summer storms.

    Dwarf types like Teddy Bear are a good choice for windy sites and containers.

    Harvesting seeds

    For seeds, the head is ready when the back turns from green to yellow-brown and the petals have dropped. Birds and squirrels know before you do, so cover heads with paper bags or mesh once seeds start to fill. Cut the head with a foot of stem and hang it in a dry, airy place to finish. West of the Cascades, fall rain can mold heads left outside, so bring them in early.

    Common questions about growing sunflowers

    Do sunflowers really follow the sun?

    Young plants do: their buds and leaves turn from east to west during the day and reset overnight. Once the flowers open, most mature heads stop moving and stay facing roughly east.

    Why are my sunflower heads empty of seeds?

    Poor pollination is the usual cause, from too few bees or cool, wet weather at bloom. Some cutting varieties like ProCut are bred to be pollenless so they don't shed on tables, and they make few seeds.

    Can I transplant sunflower seedlings?

    It's possible if you move them very young and keep the root ball intact, but they often stall. Sowing directly in the garden usually gives stronger, faster plants.
