      How to grow Oregon iris

      1. Pick a spot in sun or light shade with soil that drains well. Dry summer slopes and meadow edges suit it.
      2. Plant Oregon iris plants on your city's dates, 12 to 18 inches apart. Same depth as in the pot; rhizomes just below the soil.
      3. Water through the first summer only. It needs no fertilizer.
      4. Harvest: Enjoy the flowers in late spring. Leave the leaves standing through summer; trim old ones in late winter.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      A tough native iris

      Oregon iris, Iris tenax, grows wild in meadows, open woods and roadsides in western Oregon and southwestern Washington. Its flowers are usually lavender to deep purple, but white, cream and yellow forms occur.

      The narrow, grassy leaves form a tidy clump. They're so tough that tenax means tenacious; people once used the leaf fibers to make cord.

      Planting

      Plant in full sun to light shade in soil that drains well. It handles clay if water doesn't stand on it, and it likes dry summers. Water through the first summer only.

      Pacific Coast hybrid irises are garden hybrids of Oregon iris and its relatives, with a wider range of colors. They need the same care.

      Moving and dividing

      Oregon iris dislikes being disturbed. Clumps dug in spring or summer often die. If you need to divide it, do it in fall after the rains start, when new white roots are growing.

      Trim old, dead leaves in late winter. It needs no fertilizer.

      Common questions about growing Oregon iris

      When should I plant Oregon iris in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 1 – Apr 26 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 1 – May 27 in Spokane, Mar 20 – May 15 in Boise and Apr 28 – Jun 23 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      When does Oregon iris bloom?

      From April to June, depending on elevation and how warm the spring is.

      Can I divide Oregon iris?

      Yes, but only in fall after the rains start, when new roots are growing. Divisions made in spring or summer often fail.

      Does Oregon iris need water in summer?

      Not once established. It's adapted to dry summers, and summer watering can rot it.
