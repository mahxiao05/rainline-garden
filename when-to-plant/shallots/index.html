When to plant shallots in Oregon and Washington

Shallots go in during fall across the Northwest, or as early in spring as the soil can be worked. Find your city below.

Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun
Spacing
6 to 8 inches apart
Planting depth
Tip just at the soil surface
Frost
Very hardy; grows through winter

Planted like garlic in fall, or as early in spring as the soil can be worked. Each bulb multiplies into a cluster.

How to grow shallots

  1. Buy shallot bulbs sold for planting from a garden center or seed company.
  2. Loosen the soil and mix in an inch or two of compost.
  3. Plant them on your city's dates, 6 to 8 inches apart, with the tip just at the soil surface.
  4. Mulch lightly with straw for winter, pull weeds in spring and stop watering when the tops start to yellow.
  5. Harvest: Dig in summer when the tops yellow and fall over. Dry the clusters in a shady, airy spot for two weeks.

Varieties that do well in the Northwest

  • French Gray (gourmet, very hardy)
  • Ambition
  • Dutch Yellow

Watch out for

Onion maggots in cool, wet soil. Plant onion-family crops in a different spot each year.

What you'll need

  • Shallot bulbs for planting
  • Straw mulch
