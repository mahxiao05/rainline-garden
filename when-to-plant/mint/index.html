      How to grow mint

      1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
      2. Plant young mint plants on your city's dates, One plant per pot, or 18 inches apart. Plant at the depth it grew in the nursery pot.
      3. Water regularly; mint likes moist soil. Cut it back hard in midsummer for a fresh flush of leaves.
      4. Harvest: Snip stems just above a pair of leaves. Regular cutting keeps plants bushy.

      The plant science

      The plant biology behind the advice above, in plain language.

      Why mint takes over

      Mint spreads by underground stems called rhizomes, plus runners above ground. Each piece can root and start a new plant, so it quickly spreads through a bed. Grow it in a container, or sink a bottomless pot or bucket into the ground with the rim a couple of inches above the soil, and trim runners that creep over the edge.

      Growing from seed doesn't work well

      Peppermint is a natural hybrid between watermint and spearmint and rarely produces viable seed. Many mint seed packets produce plants with weak or unpredictable flavor. Buy plants or take cuttings from a mint whose smell and taste you like. Cuttings root easily in water.

      Flavor

      Peppermint's cool bite comes mainly from menthol; spearmint's sweeter flavor comes from carvone. Plants grown in full sun and harvested just before flowering have the most oil. Oregon and Washington, especially the Willamette Valley and the Columbia Basin, grow a large share of the country's peppermint oil.

      Soil and water

      Mint likes moist, fertile soil with a pH of about 6.0 to 7.0 and grows in sun or part shade. Containers dry out fast in summer, so water them often. Cut plants back hard in midsummer for a fresh flush of tender leaves.

      Mint rust and other problems

      Mint rust causes orange pustules on the undersides of leaves. Cut infected stems to the ground and throw them out, which removes the spores. Over the years, the center of a clump tends to die out. Divide and replant the healthy outer pieces every few years in fresh soil.

      Good neighbors

      Good neighbors

      • A pot of its own. Mint spreads by underground runners and overruns other plants.

      Keep apart

      • Open garden beds. It takes over within a year or two and is hard to dig out.

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with mint in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Mint rust

      Signs: Orange spots under the leaves in spring.

      What to do: Cut plants to the ground and throw away the cuttings; clean regrowth follows.

      Spreading

      Signs: Runners pop up in nearby beds.

      What to do: Keep the pot off the soil, and trim runners that hang over the edge.

      Woody, tired pots

      Signs: Fewer leaves after two or three years.

      What to do: Repot a few rooted pieces into fresh mix in spring.

      Common questions about growing mint

      When should I plant mint in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 15 – May 10 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 15 – Jun 10 in Spokane, Apr 1 – May 27 in Boise and May 12 – Jul 7 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      Can I grow mint from seed?

      Not reliably. Peppermint rarely makes viable seed, and many seed-grown mints have weak flavor. Buy a plant you've smelled or tasted, or root a cutting in water.

      What are the orange spots on my mint?

      Mint rust, a fungal disease. Cut the plant to the ground, throw out the clippings, and let it regrow. Good airflow and watering at the base help.

      How do I stop mint from spreading?

      Grow it in a pot, or in a bottomless container sunk into the ground with the rim a couple of inches above the soil. Trim any runners that grow over the edge.
