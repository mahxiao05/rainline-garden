    How to grow celery

    1. Start seeds indoors on your city's dates in trays or small pots under a grow light or in your sunniest window. Or skip this step and buy young plants (starts) at a garden center.
    2. Harden off the young plants: about a week before planting, set them outside for a few hours a day, a little longer each day.
    3. Plant them in the garden on your city's dates, 10 to 12 inches apart. Plant starts at the depth they grew in the pot.
    4. Water deeply once or twice a week rather than a little every day, and mulch once the soil is warm.
    5. Harvest: Cut outer stalks as you need them, or cut the whole plant at soil level once the stalks are full-sized, usually from late summer into fall.

    Watch out for

    Bolting and stringy stalks. Cold nights right after planting make it go to seed, and dry spells make it tough and bitter. Plant after nights warm up and water deeply every week.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    Starting celery from seed

    Celery seed is tiny and slow. Sow it indoors 10 to 12 weeks before planting time, pressing it onto the surface of the mix without covering it, since it needs light to sprout. Keep it at about 70°F; it can take two to three weeks to come up. Seedlings grow slowly at first, so be patient. Buying starts is a good shortcut.

    Why timing matters

    Celery is a biennial: it normally makes leaves one year and flowers the next. A week or more of nights below about 50°F convinces a young plant it has been through winter, and it bolts to seed in summer instead of making stalks. That's why the planting dates here wait for warmer nights than other cool-season crops.

    Harden seedlings off gradually, and if a cold snap comes after planting, cover them with row cover.

    Water, food and blanching

    Celery has shallow roots and comes from marshy ground, so it needs water every week of summer. Dry soil gives you stringy, bitter, hollow stalks. Mulch the bed and use a soaker hose. Feed with compost at planting and a balanced fertilizer once a month.

    Blanching (wrapping stalks in paper or cardboard for the last two weeks) makes them paler and milder, but it's optional. Many gardeners prefer the darker, stronger-flavored green stalks for cooking.

    Common questions about growing celery

    Why is my celery bitter and stringy?

    Usually not enough water or heat stress. Celery needs steady moisture all season; mulch and water deeply every week.

    Why did my celery flower in its first summer?

    It got too many cold nights while young. Wait until nights stay above about 50°F before planting out.

    Can I regrow celery from the base?

    A store-bought base will sprout leaves in water or soil, but it rarely makes full stalks. It's a fun project, not a crop.
