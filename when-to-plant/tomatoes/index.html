When to plant tomatoes in Oregon and Washington

In Portland: Start tomatoes from seed indoors Mar 15–29, then plant the young plants in the garden May 10 – Jun 7. In Bend: Jun 9 – Jul 7. Find your city below.

Difficulty
Moderate
Sun
Full sun; the hottest spot you have
Spacing
2 feet apart
Planting depth
Bury the stem up to the first leaves
Frost
Killed by frost
First harvest
About 75 days after planting

Wait for warm soil and mild nights before planting out. Early or cherry varieties ripen most reliably here.

How to grow tomatoes

  1. Start seeds indoors on your city's dates in trays or small pots under a grow light or in your sunniest window. Or skip this step and buy young plants (starts) at a garden center.
  2. Harden off the young plants: about a week before planting, set them outside for a few hours a day, a little longer each day.
  3. Plant them in the garden on your city's dates, 2 feet apart. Bury the stem up to the first leaves.
  4. Water deeply once or twice a week rather than a little every day, and mulch once the soil is warm.
  5. Harvest: Pick when fully colored. Before fall rains, pick green tomatoes and ripen them indoors.

Varieties that do well in the Northwest

  • Stupice
  • Early Girl
  • Sungold (cherry)
  • Oregon Spring (bred in Oregon)

Watch out for

Late blight in wet falls turns leaves and fruit brown. Keep leaves dry and give plants space.
