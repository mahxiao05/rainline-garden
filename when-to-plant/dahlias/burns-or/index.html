When to plant dahlias in Burns, OR

Plant dahlia tubers in Burns Jun 11 – Jul 9.

Moderate Plant tubers

Why these dates

Burns's typical last spring frost is June 11. Frost kills dahlias, so planting starts once frost risk drops, around June 11. Keep row cover or a frost blanket handy for the first couple of weeks, since a late frost is still possible. The season here is short for dahlias: start tubers in pots indoors about a month early, choose smaller early-blooming varieties, and cover plants on cool nights.

High desert and mountains. Central and high eastern Oregon. Frost is possible almost any month. Use row covers, wall-o-water or cloches for warm crops, and choose short-season varieties.

Step by step

  1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
  2. Plant dahlia tubers Jun 11 – Jul 9, 18 to 24 inches apart. 4 to 6 inches deep, eye pointing up.
  3. Don't water tubers until shoots appear, then water deeply once a week. Stake tall varieties when you plant so you don't spear the tuber later.
  4. Harvest: Cut flowers when they're almost fully open, early in the morning. The more you cut, the more they bloom.

Varieties that do well here

  • Café au Lait
  • Bishop of Llandaff
  • Cornel Brons

Watch out for

Slugs and earwigs chew young shoots and petals. Use iron phosphate slug bait early in the season.

Tubers rot in cold, wet soil, so wait until it warms. West of the Cascades many gardeners leave them in the ground over winter under thick mulch; east of the mountains, dig and store them after the first frost.

What you'll need

Common questions

When is the last frost in Burns, OR?

Typically June 11. In 9 years out of 10, frost is over by June 28. These are 1991–2020 NOAA climate normals for Burns Municipal Airport.

Is it too late to plant dahlias in Burns?

The spring planting window runs through July 9. Later plantings may not have time to bloom well before the first fall frost, which usually arrives around September 6.

When do dahlias bloom?

Blooms from midsummer until the first frost. Cut flowers when they're almost fully open, early in the morning. The more you cut, the more they bloom.
