When to plant strawberries in Oregon and Washington

In Portland: Plant strawberry plants Mar 15 – Apr 26. In Bend: May 12 – Jun 23. Find your city below.

Difficulty
Easy
Sun
Full sun
Spacing
12 to 18 inches apart
Planting depth
Crown at soil level: not buried, roots covered
Frost
Hardy perennial; comes back every year
First harvest
A light crop the first summer; full crops from the second year

June-bearers give one big crop; everbearers and day-neutrals give smaller harvests through summer. Oregon and Washington breeding programs have produced many of the best varieties for this climate.

How to grow strawberries

  1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
  2. Plant strawberry plants on your city's dates, 12 to 18 inches apart. Crown at soil level: not buried, roots covered.
  3. Water regularly, mulch with straw, and pinch off runners in the first year so plants put energy into roots.
  4. Harvest: Pick fully red berries every couple of days, in the morning, with the cap attached.

Varieties that do well in the Northwest

  • Hood (an Oregon classic)
  • Shuksan
  • Seascape (everbearing)
  • Albion (day-neutral)

Watch out for

Gray mold (botrytis) in wet springs and slugs on ripe berries. Mulch with straw to keep fruit off the soil.

What you'll need

  • Strawberry plants (bare root)
  • Straw mulch
  • Bird netting
