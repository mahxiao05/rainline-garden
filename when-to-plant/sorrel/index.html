    When to plant sorrel

    Plant in the garden: Portland and Seattle Mar 1 – Apr 26; Spokane Apr 1 – May 27; Boise Mar 20 – May 15; Bend Apr 28 – Jun 23.

    Your dates in

      Sorrel is a hardy leafy green in the buckwheat family whose sour, lemony taste comes from oxalic acid. It's cooked into soups and sauces, especially with fish and eggs, or used young and raw in salads. French (buckler-leaf) and garden sorrel are the usual kinds for eating.

      How to grow sorrel

      1. Prepare the bed: loosen the soil and mix in an inch or two of compost.
      2. Plant seeds on your town's dates, ½ inch deep, Thin to 12 inches apart.
      3. Keep the soil evenly moist until sprouts appear, which can take one to three weeks in cool soil.
      4. Harvest: Pick young leaves from early spring on; they're the most tender and least sour. Cut the plant back hard in midsummer for a fresh flush of leaves in fall.

      Growing sorrel in your part of the Northwest

      The same plant behaves differently on the wet west side, the foggy coast and the dry, colder east side.

      West-side valleys

      Willamette Valley, Puget Sound, southern Oregon

      Sorrel greens up very early in spring and keeps going into fall. Cut off flower stalks so it keeps making leaves.

      East of the Cascades

      Columbia Basin, Yakima, Boise, Spokane, north Idaho

      Sorrel likes afternoon shade and steady water in hot summers.

      High desert and mountain towns

      Central Oregon, high eastern Oregon valleys, McCall, Salmon

      Sorrel is very hardy and one of the first greens of spring.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      A lemony perennial green

      Garden sorrel and French sorrel are hardy perennials grown for their sharp, lemony leaves. They're among the first greens to grow each spring, often weeks before anything you sowed is ready, and they come back for years.

      The sour taste comes from oxalic acid, the same compound in spinach and rhubarb. It's fine in normal kitchen amounts, but people prone to kidney stones may want to go easy.

      Planting and care

      Sow seeds in early spring ½ inch deep, or plant young plants, about a foot apart. Sorrel takes sun or part shade and isn't fussy about soil. A small clump of three or four plants is plenty for most kitchens.

      Cut off flower stalks as soon as they appear in late spring; once plants flower, leaves turn tough and more sour. Divide crowded clumps every few years in spring or fall.

      Harvest through the season

      Pick young leaves, 3 to 6 inches long, which are the most tender. In midsummer heat the leaves get tough; cut the whole plant back to a couple of inches, water it, and a fresh flush grows for fall.

      French sorrel has smaller, shield-shaped leaves and a milder, more lemony taste. Red-veined sorrel is mostly ornamental, with young leaves that are good in salads.

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with sorrel in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Flower stalks

      Signs: Tall stalks make leaves tough.

      What to do: Cut them off at the base.

      Slugs and aphids

      Signs: Holes and clusters on leaves.

      What to do: Slug bait, and hose off aphids.

      A health note

      Signs: Sorrel is high in oxalic acid, like spinach.

      What to do: Fine in normal amounts; people prone to kidney stones may want to go easy.

      Common questions about growing sorrel

      When should I plant sorrel in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 1 – Apr 26 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 1 – May 27 in Spokane, Mar 20 – May 15 in Boise and Apr 28 – Jun 23 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      Does sorrel come back every year?

      Yes. Garden and French sorrel are hardy perennials that die back in hard winters and regrow in early spring.

      Why are my sorrel leaves bitter or tough?

      The plant is flowering, the leaves are old, or it's midsummer. Cut off flower stalks, pick young leaves, and cut the plant back in summer for fresh growth.

      How do you use sorrel?

      Raw in salads for a lemony bite, or cooked into soups and sauces, especially with fish and eggs. It turns olive green when cooked, which is normal.
