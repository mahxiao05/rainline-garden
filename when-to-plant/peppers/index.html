When to plant peppers in Oregon and Washington

In Portland: Start peppers from seed indoors Mar 1–15, then plant the young plants in the garden May 10–31. In Bend: Jun 9–30. Find your city below.

Difficulty
Tricky here
Sun
Full sun; against a warm wall if possible
Spacing
18 inches apart
Planting depth
Plant starts at the depth they grew in the pot
Frost
Killed by frost
First harvest
About 75 days after planting

Even more heat-hungry than tomatoes. A warm wall or black plastic mulch helps.

How to grow peppers

  1. Start seeds indoors on your city's dates in trays or small pots under a grow light or in your sunniest window. Or skip this step and buy young plants (starts) at a garden center.
  2. Harden off the young plants: about a week before planting, set them outside for a few hours a day, a little longer each day.
  3. Plant them in the garden on your city's dates, 18 inches apart. Plant starts at the depth they grew in the pot.
  4. Water deeply once or twice a week rather than a little every day, and mulch once the soil is warm.
  5. Harvest: Pick green any time once full size, or leave them to ripen red, which can take three more weeks.

Varieties that do well in the Northwest

  • Gypsy
  • Ace
  • Early Jalapeño
  • Lunchbox snacking peppers

Watch out for

Cool nights stall growth. Black plastic mulch and row cover early in the season make a big difference here.

What you'll need

  • Pepper seeds for short seasons
  • Black plastic mulch
  • Seedling heat mat
