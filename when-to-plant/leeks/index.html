    How to grow leeks

    1. Start seeds indoors on your city's dates in trays or small pots under a grow light or in your sunniest window. Or skip this step and buy young plants (starts) at a garden center.
    2. Harden off the young plants: about a week before planting, set them outside for a few hours a day, a little longer each day.
    3. Plant them in the garden on your city's dates, 6 inches apart. Set seedlings in a hole about 6 inches deep so the stems grow long and white.
    4. Water deeply once or twice a week rather than a little every day, and mulch once the soil is warm.
    5. Harvest: Dig whenever stems are an inch or more across. West of the Cascades you can leave them in the ground and harvest all winter.

    Varieties that do well here

    • King Richard (early)
    • Bandit (winter-hardy)
    • American Flag

    Watch out for

    Weeds crowd young leeks. Mulch and weed regularly while the plants are small.

    Growing notes

    What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

    Soil and feeding

    Leeks like deep, loose, fertile soil with a pH of about 6.0 to 7.5. They are heavy feeders over a long season, so work in compost before planting and side-dress with nitrogen a couple of times through summer. Their roots are shallow and their leaves don't shade out weeds, so keep the bed weeded and mulched. Even moisture matters: dry spells slow growth and make stems tough.

    Growing long, white stems

    The white part of a leek is simply stem that grew in the dark. Setting seedlings in a 6-inch-deep hole, as the guide describes, starts the process. Don't fill the hole; water settles soil around the plant over time. As leeks grow, you can hill soil or mulch up around the stems for more white length. Keep soil out of the leaf folds, where it is hard to wash out. King Richard is a quick summer and fall leek, while Bandit and American Flag hold up through winter.

    Winter harvest and problems

    West of the Cascades, hardy leeks stand in the garden all winter and can be dug as needed, which makes them one of the best winter vegetables in the region. East of the Cascades, harsh cold may damage them, so harvest before the deep freeze or cover them heavily with straw. Rust, small orange spots on the leaves, sometimes appears in late summer. It looks bad but rarely ruins the stems; pick off affected leaves and give plants more space. Onion thrips can silver the leaves in hot, dry eastern summers.

    Common questions about growing leeks

    How do I get long white leeks?

    Plant seedlings deep, in holes about 6 inches deep, and hill soil or mulch around the stems as they grow. The part kept from light stays white and tender. Rich soil and steady water help them grow thick.

    Can I leave leeks in the ground over winter?

    West of the Cascades, yes: winter-hardy varieties like Bandit can be dug all winter. East of the Cascades, mulch them heavily or harvest before hard freezes. Dig them before they send up flower stalks in spring.

    What are the orange spots on my leek leaves?

    That's rust, a fungal disease common in late summer. It's mostly cosmetic and the stems are still good to eat. Remove the worst leaves, avoid crowding, and don't plant leeks or garlic in the same spot next year.
