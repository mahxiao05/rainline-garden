    When to plant parsnips

    Plant in the garden: Portland and Seattle Mar 15 – Apr 26; Spokane Apr 15 – May 27; Boise Apr 1 – May 13; Bend May 12 – Jun 23.

    Your dates in

      Parsnips are a long, cream-colored root in the carrot family, sweeter and nuttier than carrots, roasted, mashed or added to soups and stews. They take most of the growing season, around four months, to size up. Wear gloves when handling the leaves on sunny days: parsnip sap can cause skin burns in sunlight.

      How to grow parsnips

      1. Prepare the bed: loosen the soil and mix in an inch or two of compost.
      2. Plant seeds on your town's dates, ½ inch deep, Thin to 3 to 4 inches apart.
      3. Keep the soil evenly moist until sprouts appear, which can take one to three weeks in cool soil.
      4. Harvest: Dig roots from late fall through winter, after a few frosts have sweetened them. West of the Cascades they can stay in the ground until early spring.

      Growing parsnips in your part of the Northwest

      The same plant behaves differently on the wet west side, the foggy coast and the dry, colder east side.

      West-side valleys

      Willamette Valley, Puget Sound, southern Oregon

      Parsnips are slow to sprout, so keep the soil moist for up to three weeks. Many gardeners leave them in the ground and dig through winter, since frost sweetens them.

      Coast

      Oregon and Washington coast

      Parsnips grow well in the long, cool season, and sandy soils give straight roots.

      East of the Cascades

      Columbia Basin, Yakima, Boise, Spokane, north Idaho

      Dry wind crusts the soil surface, so keep the seedbed moist until seedlings appear. Mulch the row heavily if you leave roots in over winter.

      High desert and mountain towns

      Central Oregon, high eastern Oregon valleys, McCall, Salmon

      Parsnips handle cold well and get sweeter after frost. Mulch them if you plan to harvest after the ground starts freezing.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      Getting parsnips to sprout

      Most parsnip failures happen before the seedlings appear. The seed is short-lived, so buy fresh seed every year. It takes two to four weeks to sprout in cool spring soil, and the bed has to stay moist the whole time. A light board or burlap laid over the row, lifted as soon as you see green, helps keep the surface from crusting.

      Sow a few radish seeds in the same row. They come up fast, mark where the row is and break the crust for the slower parsnips.

      Soil for long roots

      Parsnips grow a root 8 to 12 inches long, so they need deep, loose soil with no rocks or lumps of fresh manure, which cause forked roots. Raised beds or deeply dug soil work best in heavy Northwest clay. They're not heavy feeders; well-rotted compost dug in before sowing is enough.

      Frost, sweetness and winter storage

      Parsnips are a long-season crop meant to be dug after cold weather arrives. A few frosts turn their stored starch into sugar, and the roots get much sweeter. West of the Cascades, just leave them in the ground and dig as needed through winter. East of the Cascades, mulch the row heavily with straw so the ground doesn't freeze solid, or dig and store them in a cold, humid fridge drawer.

      Wear gloves and long sleeves when handling the leaves on sunny days: the sap can make skin blister when it's exposed to sunlight.

      Good neighbors and crop rotation

      Good neighbors

      • Radishes. Sow a few with parsnip seed. They mark the row while the slow parsnips sprout.
      • Onions. Narrow leaves that share the bed well.

      Carrot family: Carrots, parsnips, celery, parsley, dill and cilantro share pests. Carrot rust fly pupae overwinter in the soil where last year's carrots grew, so plant the next crop as far away as your garden allows and rotate on a three-year cycle.

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with parsnips in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Poor sprouting

      Signs: Few seedlings after weeks.

      What to do: Use fresh seed each year and keep the bed moist for up to four weeks.

      Carrot rust fly

      Signs: Rusty tunnels in the roots.

      What to do: Insect netting and rotating beds.

      Sap burns

      Signs: Rashes or blisters after handling leaves on a sunny day.

      What to do: Wear gloves and long sleeves when working around the leaves.

      Common questions about growing parsnips

      When should I plant parsnips in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 15 – Apr 26 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 15 – May 27 in Spokane, Apr 1 – May 13 in Boise and May 12 – Jun 23 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      Why didn't my parsnips come up?

      Usually old seed or a seedbed that dried out during the long sprouting time. Use seed packed for this year and keep the surface moist for up to four weeks.

      When should I dig parsnips?

      From late fall onward, after a few frosts. In mild west-side gardens they can stay in the ground until early spring, when they start to regrow.

      Why are my parsnips forked?

      Rocks, hard soil or fresh manure in the bed. Loosen the soil deeply and use only well-aged compost.
