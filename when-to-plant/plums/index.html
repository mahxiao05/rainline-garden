    When to plant plum trees

    Plant in the garden: Portland and Seattle Mar 1 – Apr 26; Spokane Apr 1 – May 27; Boise Mar 20 – May 15; Bend Apr 28 – Jun 23.

    Your dates in

      Plum trees are small fruit trees grown for fresh plums, jam and drying. European plums are oval, firm and sweet, and the best for drying; Japanese plums are round, juicy and often red or yellow. A prune is simply a European plum with enough sugar to dry without fermenting around the pit.

      How to grow plum trees

      1. Pick a sunny spot with soil that drains well, and mix in an inch or two of compost.
      2. Plant plum trees on your town's dates, 12 to 15 feet apart for dwarf or semi-dwarf trees. Graft union 2 to 3 inches above the soil.
      3. Water deeply through the first two summers. Prune lightly in summer, not winter, which lowers the risk of silver leaf and canker. Thin heavy crops so branches don't snap.
      4. Harvest: Pick when the fruit is fully colored, slightly soft and comes off easily. Prune plums for drying should hang until they wrinkle slightly at the stem.

      Growing plum trees in your part of the Northwest

      The same plant behaves differently on the wet west side, the foggy coast and the dry, colder east side.

      West-side valleys

      Willamette Valley, Puget Sound, southern Oregon

      Italian prune plums have been grown in the Willamette Valley for over a century. Brown rot spreads in wet weather near harvest; pick off and throw away rotting fruit.

      East of the Cascades

      Columbia Basin, Yakima, Boise, Spokane, north Idaho

      Plums grow well with irrigation, but they bloom early, and a late spring frost can wipe out the crop.

      High desert and mountain towns

      Central Oregon, high eastern Oregon valleys, McCall, Salmon

      European and Japanese plums often lose blossoms to frost. Hardy American and hybrid plums are more dependable.

      Growing notes

      What makes the difference in Northwest gardens, in plain language.

      European or Japanese plums

      European plums, including Italian prune and Stanley, bloom later and fruit on their own, which makes them the easiest choice for most Northwest gardens. Their dense, sweet flesh is great fresh, dried or baked. Japanese plums such as Shiro and Methley bloom earlier, have juicier fruit and ripen in mid-summer. Shiro sets a much better crop with another Japanese plum nearby, and Methley is a popular partner because it's partly self-fertile. In frost-prone spots, the earlier bloom of Japanese plums can get caught by a cold snap.

      Why plums are pruned in summer

      Plums and other stone fruits are prone to silver leaf and bacterial canker, which enter through pruning cuts in cool, wet weather. Cuts made in dry summer weather heal fast and stay clean. Prune lightly after harvest or in July, removing dead wood, crossing branches and tall upright shoots.

      Heavy crops are common. Thin young fruit to 3 to 4 inches apart in early summer: the remaining plums grow bigger, brown rot spreads less between touching fruit, and branches are less likely to snap.

      Harvest and brown rot

      Brown rot is the main Northwest plum problem. It blights blossoms in rainy springs and turns ripening fruit into fuzzy gray balls that dry into mummies. Those mummies carry the fungus into next year, so pick them off the tree and the ground. Good airflow and thinning help a lot.

      Italian prunes are sweetest when left to hang until they wrinkle slightly at the stem. Japanese plums soften quickly once ripe, so check every couple of days.

      Pests and problems

      What most often goes wrong with plum trees in Northwest gardens, how to spot it and what to do.

      Brown rot

      Signs: Fuzzy gray rot on ripening fruit.

      What to do: Thin fruit and remove mummies.

      Aphids

      Signs: Curled leaves on new shoots.

      What to do: Hose off; predators usually follow.

      Bacterial canker

      Signs: Gum oozing from branches in spring.

      What to do: Prune in dry summer weather, not winter.

      Common questions about growing plum trees

      When should I plant plum trees in Oregon, Washington and Idaho?

      Plant in the garden: Mar 1 – Apr 26 in Portland and Seattle, Apr 1 – May 27 in Spokane, Mar 20 – May 15 in Boise and Apr 28 – Jun 23 in Bend. Pick your town at the top of the page for your own dates.

      Do plum trees need a pollinator?

      European plums like Italian prune and Stanley are self-fertile and fruit fine alone. Japanese plums such as Shiro do much better with another Japanese plum nearby.

      What's the difference between a prune and a plum?

      A prune plum is a European plum with dense, sweet flesh that dries well without fermenting at the pit. Italian prune is the classic Northwest variety, and it's just as good eaten fresh.

      Why are my plums rotting on the tree?

      That's usually brown rot, a fungus spread by wet weather. Thin the fruit so plums don't touch, remove any shriveled mummies from the tree and the ground, and pick promptly when ripe.
